Meel Group

University of Toronto

Welcome to the Meel Group’s web page. Our group is based at the University of Toronto and the Georgia Institute of Technology.

Led by Kuldeep S. Meel, we develop scalable automated reasoning techniques that bridge theory and practice. Our research draws on randomized algorithms, statistical inference, formal methods, and distribution testing.

  • Constrained Sampling and Counting

Research

Our primary research interest is in automated reasoning. The long term vision of our research program is to advance automated reasoning techniques to enable computing to deal with increasingly uncertain real-world environments. The core theme of our research program is the quest for scalability. Accordingly, our work straddles theory and practice, and draws upon ideas from randomized algorithms, statistical inference, formal methods, distribution testing, and software engineering.

Our research spans multiple areas of computer science, as reflected in our publication record through 2026: artificial intelligence and machine learning (AAAI: 27×, IJCAI: 14×, NeurIPS: 6×, KR: 4×, AISTATS: 3×, ICML: 1×, ICLR: 1×), formal methods and constraint solving (CAV: 12×, SAT: 10×, CP: 9×, LPAR: 4×, TACAS: 3×), databases (PODS: 6×, ICDT: 2×), design automation (DAC: 3×, DATE: 3×, ICCAD: 2×), and theoretical computer science (LICS: 2×, SODA: 1×, ICALP: 1×, ESA: 1×). These connections allow us to bring advances in algorithms and reasoning systems to problems across disciplines.

Two of our papers were accepted to LPAR-26, held in Spetses, Greece, on October 25–30, 2026:

  • Scaling Zero Knowledge UNSAT Verification via Normalized Chaining — Ashwin Karthikeyan, Ethan Kharitonov, Kuldeep S. Meel, and Anwar Hithnawi. The work improves the scalability of zero-knowledge UNSAT certification by normalizing resolution proofs to reduce prover memory requirements.
  • Formally Solving Answer-Construction Problems in Lean — Jialiang Sun, Yuzhi Tang, Ao Li, Chris Maddison, and Kuldeep Meel. The work introduces Enumerate-Conjecture-Prove, combining answer construction with machine-checked proofs and admissibility checks in Lean.

Openings

Our group is based at the University of Toronto and the Georgia Institute of Technology. We work at the intersection of algorithm design and systems, with an emphasis on scalable automated reasoning. We welcome applicants with a strong background in algorithms, statistics, or formal methods and experience building research software.
